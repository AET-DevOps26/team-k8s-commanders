# Acute Cough and Upper Respiratory Tract Infection

> Illustrative reference summary for the CareDesk demo. Simplified; not a
> substitute for a current, authoritative clinical guideline.

## Assessment

- Acute cough is usually caused by a self-limiting viral upper respiratory tract
  infection and typically resolves within 3–4 weeks.
- Assess for features suggesting a more serious cause: haemoptysis, breathlessness
  at rest, chest pain, weight loss, or a cough persisting beyond 8 weeks — these
  warrant further investigation (e.g. chest imaging).
- Check for signs of pneumonia (focal chest signs, high fever, tachypnoea) which
  changes management.

## Management

- For an otherwise well adult, advise self-care: rest, fluids, and that the cough
  is expected to settle without antibiotics.
- Honey or over-the-counter cough preparations may give modest symptomatic relief.
- Do not routinely offer antibiotics for an acute cough associated with a simple
  viral infection; they do not meaningfully shorten the illness and risk harms.
- Consider antibiotics only when there is clinical suspicion of pneumonia or the
  patient is systemically very unwell or at higher risk of complications.

## Safety-netting

- Advise the patient to seek review if symptoms worsen rapidly, breathing becomes
  difficult, they cough up blood, or the cough has not improved after 3–4 weeks.
