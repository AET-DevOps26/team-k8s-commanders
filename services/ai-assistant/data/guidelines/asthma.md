# Asthma — Diagnosis and Pharmacological Management

> Illustrative reference summary for the CareDesk demo. Simplified; not a
> substitute for a current, authoritative clinical guideline.

## Diagnosis

- Base a diagnosis on a history of variable respiratory symptoms (wheeze,
  breathlessness, chest tightness, cough) plus objective evidence of variable
  airflow obstruction.
- Supporting tests include spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility,
  fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO), and peak-flow variability.

## Pharmacological management

- Do not treat with a short-acting beta agonist (SABA) alone; everyone should
  receive an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)-containing regimen.
- A low-dose ICS/formoterol combination used as needed (anti-inflammatory
  reliever) is preferred to a SABA reliever for mild asthma.
- Step up to regular low-dose ICS/formoterol maintenance-and-reliever therapy if
  symptoms are not controlled.
- Review inhaler technique and adherence before escalating treatment.

## Monitoring and review

- Assess control at every review using symptom frequency, reliever use, and any
  exacerbations.
- Provide every patient with a personalised written asthma action plan.
