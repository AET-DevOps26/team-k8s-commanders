# Hypertension — Diagnosis and Management

> Illustrative reference summary for the CareDesk demo. Simplified; not a
> substitute for a current, authoritative clinical guideline.

## Diagnosis

- Diagnose hypertension when clinic blood pressure is 140/90 mmHg or higher AND
  ambulatory or home readings average 135/85 mmHg or higher.
- Stage 1: clinic 140/90–159/99 mmHg. Stage 2: clinic 160/100 mmHg or higher.
- Confirm an elevated clinic reading with ambulatory (ABPM) or home (HBPM)
  monitoring before starting lifelong treatment, unless blood pressure is very
  high (e.g. 180/120 mmHg) or there is evidence of target-organ damage.

## Lifestyle advice (offer to all patients)

- Reduce dietary sodium; encourage a diet rich on vegetables and fruit.
- Limit alcohol, stop smoking, and support regular physical activity.
- Reduce caffeine intake if consumption is high.

## Drug treatment

- Step 1: under 55 and not of Black African/Caribbean origin → ACE inhibitor or
  ARB. Aged 55 or over, or of Black African/Caribbean origin → calcium-channel
  blocker (CCB) such as amlodipine.
- Step 2: combine ACE inhibitor/ARB with a CCB or a thiazide-like diuretic.
- Step 3: ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB + thiazide-like diuretic.
- Review adherence, dose, and home readings before escalating.

## Targets and follow-up

- Treat to a clinic target below 140/90 mmHg (below 150/90 mmHg for adults over
  80). Use home readings (target below 135/85 mmHg) to guide adjustment.
- Recheck blood pressure and renal function after starting or changing an
  ACE inhibitor/ARB or diuretic.
