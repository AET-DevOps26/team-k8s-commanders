# Type 2 Diabetes — Initial Management

> Illustrative reference summary for the CareDesk demo. Simplified; not a
> substitute for a current, authoritative clinical guideline.

## Diagnosis

- Diagnose with HbA1c of 48 mmol/mol (6.5%) or higher, or fasting plasma glucose
  of 7.0 mmol/L or higher, confirmed on a second test in an asymptomatic person.
- An HbA1c of 42–47 mmol/mol (6.0–6.4%) indicates high risk ("prediabetes");
  offer intensive lifestyle support.

## Lifestyle and education

- Offer structured education and dietary advice at diagnosis.
- Support weight loss, increased physical activity, and smoking cessation.

## Drug treatment

- First-line: metformin, titrated slowly to limit gastrointestinal side effects;
  use the modified-release form if not tolerated.
- Consider adding an SGLT2 inhibitor for patients with established cardiovascular
  disease, heart failure, or high cardiovascular risk.
- Individualise the HbA1c target (commonly 48–53 mmol/mol) based on the risk of
  hypoglycaemia and comorbidity.

## Monitoring

- Check HbA1c every 3–6 months until stable, then every 6 months.
- Monitor renal function, blood pressure, and perform annual foot and retinal
  screening.
